## QUESTION 2: 65% EQUITY

**Proposed Rule:** 50% Stack requires 65%+ equity

### Evidence

| # | Transcript | Speaker | Context | Type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `03-STEP3-Pt1-Deal-Structure-Cash-Stack-SubTo.txt:63-67` | Jaxon | "For a property to be able to be bought that way, they must have a minimum of 50% equity, because if they have more than that and we put $50,000 down at closing, the seller's going to lose money. So we like the equity to be at least 65% of the value of the home, so that when we put 50% down, the seller's able to make money." | Instructional |
| 2 | `AIREI_MASTER_PLAYBOOK.md:236` | (compiled) | "Minimum equity required: 65% (so seller nets after your down payment)" | Instructional |

### Counter-Evidence

Jaxon's own words establish two thresholds: "must have a minimum of 50% equity" (hard floor) and "we like the equity to be at least 65%" (preferred). The Master Playbook collapses this to "Minimum equity required: 65%" which is a simplification.

### Verdict

**Preferred screening heuristic, not a universal hard requirement.**

The hard minimum is 50% equity (seller must not lose money after 50% down payment). 65% is the preferred threshold where the seller "makes money" on the deal. The Master Playbook's "Minimum equity required: 65%" is a simplification of Jaxon's more nuanced teaching.

**APPROVED_FOR_CANONICAL_OS** with clarification: Minimum 50% equity (hard floor). Preferred 65%+ equity (seller profitability threshold).

## QUESTION 5: THE 1% RULE

**Proposed Rule:** Monthly rent ~1% of purchase price

| 2 | `03-STEP3-Pt1-Deal-Structure-Cash-Stack-SubTo.txt:21-25` | Jaxon | "So even if a property is running for the 1% rule, say there's a property running for 2 grand and it's worth $200,000, it's a turnkey property. The course presents it as a screening preference ("we like to see"), not a hard requirement. Jaxon explicitly downgrades it for fix and flips. The Master Playbook's "must" language overstates the source material.
